# Plan Irrigation Zones by Emitter Flow and Crop Demand

## Group by watering requirement before counting valves

Record crop, growth stage, root medium, container size where applicable, and the area served. Colorado State notes that scheduling depends on plant need, soil and season and distinguishes microsprays from drip zones.

Use that principle to flag groups needing different schedules. Do not derive commercial crop demand from a garden example or assign one daily duration to every crop.

- Identify who will provide the agronomic requirement.
- Mark groups that change during the season.
- Keep cooling/fogging demand separate from root-zone irrigation.

## Build a count by exact emitter group

For each group, multiply outlet count by discharge per outlet in the same units, then add groups intended to run together. If the model’s flow depends on pressure, use the value at the proposed operating point.

Arithmetic illustration: 80 outlets at 2 L/h plus 40 outlets at 4 L/h totals 320 L/h, or about 5.33 L/min. These invented counts do not specify a BLUSTAL kit, lateral capacity or crop requirement.

- Count actual outlets, not just plants.
- Record whether a multi-outlet assembly rating is per outlet or per assembly.
- Retain emitter model and data-sheet reference beside each flow.

## Compare simultaneous demand with verified supply

Ask the designer to compare the proposed simultaneous load with source flow available at the required operating pressure. A pump label, tank volume or open-tap bucket test alone is not a complete system operating-point verification.

Keep flushing, backwashing and any other simultaneous water uses visible in the brief. Have the designer state whether they operate separately or must be included in a particular operating case.

- List allowed simultaneous zone combinations.
- Show supply-test conditions and date.
- Ask the designer to identify pressure-loss assumptions.

## Keep volume arithmetic separate from the crop decision

Once the grower or agronomist establishes a target volume, nominal runtime in hours is target delivered litres divided by verified operating litres per hour. That arithmetic does not account automatically for uneven application, losses or soil intake.

For example, 160 litres divided by 320 L/h equals half an hour. It is an arithmetic example only. Do not use it as a crop schedule.

- Label target-volume source and units.
- Review moisture or drainage observations separately.
- Keep adjustments and their reasons dated.
